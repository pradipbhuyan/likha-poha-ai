2. Simple Explanation

Glass bangles are beautiful circular ornaments made from glass, often worn during festivals and special occasions in India. Their making involves heating glass in a furnace, shaping it carefully, adding colors and patterns, and then cooling it. Artisans who craft these bangles are skilled workers, and their work reflects the culture and traditions of their town. Modern designers, like Ananya, help create new styles by mixing traditional patterns with new ideas, making the bangles more attractive and popular. Bangles are not just jewelry; they are part of a rich cultural tradition that celebrates festivals like Diwali.

3. Step-by-Step Breakdown

  • Making Glass Bangles:
  • Heat glass in a furnace until it becomes soft.
  • Shape the hot glass into a circular bangle.
  • Add colors and patterns before cooling.
  • Cool the bangles to make them hard and durable.
  • Cultural Significance:
  • Bangles are an important part of Indian festivals, especially Diwali.
  • Women wear bangles to celebrate and show their culture.
  • Artisans and Craftsmanship:
  • Skilled artisans like Ravi craft bangles with attention to detail.
  • Their work involves patience and skill.
  • Modern Designs:
  • Ananya, Ravi’s daughter, introduces new patterns and colors.
  • Combining tradition with modern ideas makes bangles more attractive.
  • Impact on Community:
  • Increased demand creates more jobs.
  • The craft supports local artisans and the community.

4. Worked Example

Suppose Ravi heats glass in a furnace at 1200°C until it softens. Then, he shapes it into a circle using a mold. Before cooling, he paints traditional floral patterns on the glass. Once cooled, the bangles are polished and checked for quality. If Ravi makes 50 bangles a day, and each takes about 15 minutes, he can produce around 200 bangles in a day. This process shows how skill and patience are essential in making beautiful, long-lasting bangles.

5. Common Mistake

Many students think that once the glass is shaped, the process is over. But in reality, adding colors, patterns, and cooling are equally important steps. Missing these steps can result in bangles that are fragile or plain. Remember, every step—from heating to cooling—affects the final quality.

b) To cool it quickly.

c) To add colors.

d) To make it hard.

Answer: a) To make it soft and easy to shape.

  • They are an important part of Indian festivals and culture.
  • Skilled artisans like Ravi craft these bangles with patience and care.
  • Modern designs help make traditional crafts more attractive.
  • The craft supports local communities and preserves cultural traditions.

What you will learn

Today, we will understand how to solve a typical story-based problem involving objects like balls and scooters, as seen in the chapter "Gone with the Scooter." We will learn how to carefully analyze the problem, identify the key information, and follow logical steps to reach the solution.

Simple explanation

In story problems, the key is to read carefully, understand what is asked, and use logical reasoning or basic math to find the answer. We will see how to break down the problem into smaller parts, think about what each part means, and then combine our understanding to solve it.

Step-by-step breakdown

1. Identify the problem: Understand what the question is asking.

2. Gather information: Look at the details given in the story.

3. Decide what to find: Determine the unknown you need to solve for.

4. Plan the solution: Choose the right method or steps.

5. Solve systematically: Carry out the steps carefully.

6. Check your answer: Make sure it makes sense in the story context.

Worked example

Question:

Gopi found a hockey ball in the garden. Later, he and his friends played a game with the ball. During the game, Manoj hit the ball so hard that it went past the playground gate and fell into the basket of a passing scooter. The scooter was unaware of the ball, and by the time the children ran to the gate, the scooter was gone, and so was the ball. How can we understand what happened to the ball after it went into the scooter’s basket? What might happen next?

Step 1:

Identify what the problem asks.

It wants us to think about what happens to the ball after it falls into the scooter’s basket.

Step 2:

Gather information from the story.

  • The ball was hit hard by Manoj.
  • It went past the playground gate.
  • It fell into a passing scooter’s basket.
  • The scooter drove away, and the ball was gone.

Step 3:

What are we asked to find?

We need to imagine what might happen to the ball after it falls into the scooter’s basket.

Step 4:

Plan the solution.

Since the story doesn’t give more details, we use reasoning:

  • The ball is in the scooter’s basket.
  • The scooter is moving away.
  • The ball might fall out or stay in the basket until the scooter stops.

Step 5:

Solve systematically.

  • If the scooter keeps moving, the ball might stay in the basket until the scooter stops.
  • When the scooter stops, someone might take out the ball.
  • Alternatively, if the ball is loose, it might fall out during the ride.

Step 6:

Check your answer.

It makes sense that the ball would stay in the basket until the scooter stops or someone takes it out. This aligns with how objects behave in moving vehicles.

Conceptual interpretation:

This problem helps us understand how objects behave when they are in moving vehicles. The key idea is that objects stay in the vehicle unless acted upon by another force or unless the vehicle stops.

Common mistake

Students might think the ball automatically stays in the basket forever or that it falls out immediately. Remember, objects in a moving vehicle stay until something causes them to move or fall out.

Quick check question

If a ball falls into a moving scooter’s basket, what do you think will happen to the ball if the scooter suddenly stops? Why?

Summary

In this lesson, we learned how to analyze a story problem involving objects in motion. We identified what was asked, gathered information, planned our reasoning, and logically deduced what might happen next. Remember, understanding the story and applying logical reasoning are key to solving such problems. Review these points, then move to the next lesson step when ready.

Worked Example: Using the Present Perfect Tense

What you will learn

Today, we will learn how to use the present perfect tense correctly in sentences. We will see how it is used to talk about actions that happened recently or actions that happened in the past but still affect the present.

Simple explanation

The present perfect tense is formed by using the helping words "has" or "have" + the past participle of the main verb (usually the verb ending in -ed for regular verbs).

  • Use "has" with he, she, it.
  • Use "have" with I, you, we, they.

It is used to talk about:

  • Actions completed recently.
  • Experiences in life that are still relevant now.

Step-by-step breakdown

1. Identify the subject of the sentence (I, you, he, she, it, we, they).

2. Choose "has" or "have" based on the subject.

3. Use the past participle form of the verb. For regular verbs, add -ed to the base form.

4. Combine them to form the sentence.

Example structure:

Subject + has/have + past participle + object

---

Worked Example: Fill in the blanks

Question:

Complete the sentences using the present perfect tense.

a) I __________ (visit) Delhi many times.

b) She __________ (finish) her homework.

c) Mr. Joon __________ (buy) a new car.

d) They __________ (write) letters to their friends.

---

Step 1: Understand the sentence

  • For each sentence, identify the subject.
  • Decide whether to use "has" or "have".

Step 2: Find the correct past participle

  • visit → visited (regular verb, add -ed)
  • finish → finished
  • buy → bought (irregular verb, special form)
  • write → written (irregular verb, special form)

Step 3: Form the sentences

  • I have visited Delhi many times.
  • She has finished her homework.
  • Mr. Joon has bought a new car.
  • They have written letters to their friends.

---

Final answers:

a) I have visited Delhi many times.

b) She has finished her homework.

c) Mr. Joon has bought a new car.

d) They have written letters to their friends.

---

Why these forms?

  • "have" is used with I, you, we, they because these subjects are plural or first/second person.
  • "has" is used with he, she, it because these are singular third-person subjects.
  • The past participle shows the action completed.

Common mistake

Using "has" with I or "have" with he. Remember:

  • I have...
  • He has...

Quick check question:

Fill in the blank:

She __________ (read) the book already.

  • Present perfect tense is formed with has/have + past participle.
  • Use "have" with I, you, we, they.
  • Use "has" with he, she, it.
  • It is used to talk about recent actions or experiences that matter now.

2. Simple Explanation

Frogs love to live in places that are wet and damp, like ponds, muddy drains, and places with dew. They catch insects and small animals with their quick tongue. Frogs can move easily in water because they have webbed feet, which act like flippers. On land, they hop and jump using their strong legs. Their colors help them hide among plants and leaves, protecting them from enemies.

3. Step-by-step Breakdown

Where do frogs like to stay?

  • Frogs like to sit or spend time in damp, wet places such as ponds, muddy drains, and areas with dew.
  • These places keep them moist, which is important because frogs have moist skin.

How does the frog catch its food?

  • Frogs flick their long, sticky tongue very quickly to catch insects like mosquitoes.
  • Their tongue works so fast that they can catch food before it flies away.

How does the frog move?

  • In water: Frogs use their webbed feet, which act like flippers, to swim smoothly.
  • On land: Frogs hop or jump using their strong back legs.

What helps the frog hide?

  • Frogs have colors that match plants and trees.
  • This camouflage helps them hide from predators in grass and leaves.

4. Worked Example

Suppose a frog is sitting on a green leaf near a pond. It sees a mosquito flying. The frog flicks its tongue quickly and catches the mosquito. Then, it jumps into the pond to swim away.

Why does the frog flick its tongue?

Because its tongue is sticky and very fast, helping it catch insects easily.

Why does it jump into the pond after catching the mosquito?

Because frogs are good swimmers, and the pond is their home for swimming and hiding.

  • They help in managing water efficiently in homes and communities.

  • Underground tanks: These tanks are built below the ground and can store enough water for many months.
  • Overhead tanks: These are placed on the roofs of houses and store hundreds or thousands of liters of water. They are easy to fill and access.

  • They think about what is fair and just.
  • They give a decision that helps solve the problem fairly.
  • Everyone follows the Panchayat’s decision, which keeps peace in the village.

Why did the Panchayat make this decision?

Reasoning: Following the Panchayat’s decision helps keep peace.

This decision was fair because it respected the rights of both parties and used wisdom to solve the problem.

  • The farmer's problem: He bought a well but the neighbor refused to let him take water, saying he only sold the well, not the water.
  • The farmer's worry: He didn't know what to do because he needed water for his fields.
  • The Panchayat's role: The village elders gathered to listen to both sides and decide what was fair.
  • The decision made: The Panchayat said that if the neighbor had not sold the water, he couldn't keep it in the well. He had to remove the water or use it immediately.
  • Outcome: The neighbor realized he couldn't cheat and paid a fine and apologized. The farmer got his water, and everyone learned a lesson about honesty.
