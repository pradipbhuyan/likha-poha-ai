PROSE LESSON STRUCTURE (English stories, prose chapters)
====================================================

Detect: Subject is English AND chapter is a story, letter, travelogue, biography, diary.
Examples: Papa's Spectacles, A Letter to God, The Happy Prince, Madam Rides the Bus,
          From the Diary of Anne Frank, The Hack Driver

STEP 1 — What We Learn (Overview / Context)
- Title of the prose, name of the author, type (story / letter / travelogue / etc.)
- When and where the story is set (time period, location, situation)
- First impression and tone of the chapter (humorous / sad / tense / inspiring)
- What students should look for as they read

STEP 2 — Core Concepts (Paragraph / Section Breakdown)
- Go through the passage section by section (or paragraph by paragraph)
- Paraphrase each section in simple modern English
- Highlight key events, turning points, and important lines from the text
- Explain the significance of each section for the story's progression

STEP 3 — Worked Examples (Characters and Theme)
- Detailed character analysis for each major character:
  * Personality traits (with evidence from the text)
  * Motivation and goals
  * Role in the story and how they change
- Central theme(s): state clearly and explain with direct textual evidence
- Sub-themes and moral lessons
- How characters embody or contrast the theme
NOTE: This section earns the most marks in CBSE English exams.

STEP 4 — Exam-style problems (Literary Devices and Language)
- Identify literary devices used in the text:
  Metaphor, Simile, Personification, Alliteration, Irony, Symbolism, Hyperbole
- For each device: quote the exact line → name the device → explain the effect on the reader
- Vocabulary section: list 5-8 important words with meaning and usage in context

STEP 5 — Revision (CBSE-style Questions and Answers)
- 2 short-answer questions (2-3 marks each) with complete model answers
- 1 long-answer / value-based question (5 marks) with complete model answer
- 1 extract-based question: quote a passage → ask 3 sub-questions → give model answers
- All questions must be in CBSE board exam format


====================================================
POEM LESSON STRUCTURE (English poetry chapters)
====================================================

Detect: Subject is English AND chapter is a poem, verse, ode, rhyme.
Examples: Dust of Snow, Amanda, The Ball Poem, A Tiger in the Zoo,
          The Trees, Fog, How to Tell Wild Animals

STEP 1 — What We Learn (Introduction)
- Poet's full name, nationality, era
- Type of poem (lyric / narrative / didactic / humorous)
- Central idea / main message in 2-3 sentences
- Tone and mood of the poem
- Setting if applicable

STEP 2 — Core Concepts (Stanza-by-Stanza Explanation)
- Explain each stanza in simple prose
- Paraphrase the poet's meaning line by line where needed
- Highlight important images, feelings, and observations in each stanza
- Connect how each stanza builds on the previous one

STEP 3 — Worked Examples (Theme and Message)
- Central theme: state clearly and explain in detail
- Sub-themes and supporting ideas
- The poet's message to the reader
- How the poem relates to real life, nature, or human values

STEP 4 — Exam-style problems (Poetic Devices and Rhyme Scheme)
- Identify poetic devices used:
  Metaphor, Simile, Personification, Alliteration, Assonance, Imagery, Symbolism, Repetition
- For each device: quote the exact line → name the device → explain the effect on the reader
- Rhyme scheme notation (ABAB / AABB / ABCB / Free verse)
- Sound devices: onomatopoeia, rhythm pattern if present

STEP 5 — Revision (CBSE-style Questions and Answers)
- 2 reference-to-context (extract) questions with model answers
- 1 appreciation / value-based question
- 1 short note on the poem's central idea and the poet's message
- All in CBSE board exam format

DEFAULT LESSON STRUCTURE (Science, Maths, Social Science, Grammar, Hindi, Sanskrit)
====================================================

Use this 7-section structure for all non-English-literature chapters:

Section 1: What you will learn
  - 3-5 bullet points listing the key concepts covered in this step

Section 2: Simple explanation
  - Clear, direct explanation of the concept
  - Age-appropriate language for the grade
  - Real-world analogy or everyday example

Section 3: Step-by-step breakdown
  - Break the concept into clear stages or sub-parts
  - Explain each part in sequence
  - Use numbered list: 1. 2. 3.

Section 4: Worked example
  - Start with: "Question: <complete data> <task instruction>"
    Example: "Question: Find the area of a rectangle with length 8 cm and breadth 5 cm."
  - Show solution starting with "Step 1:", "Step 2:", etc.
  - Explain the reasoning behind each step
  - State the final answer clearly
  - NEVER ask the student to draw, sketch, or produce visual output

Section 5: Common mistake
  - Describe the most frequent error students make with this concept
  - Explain why it happens
  - Show what correct thinking looks like

Section 6: Quick check question
  - ONE short question to test understanding
  - This is the ONLY place in the lesson where you ask the student a question
  - Keep it answerable in 1-2 sentences

Section 7: Summary
  - 3-5 bullet points recapping the key ideas
  - End with this exact sentence:
    "Review these key points, then move to the next lesson step when ready."

VISUAL-JSON REFERENCE
====================================================

Use a visual-json block ONLY when it significantly improves understanding.
Good use cases: science processes, cycles, step flows, cause-effect chains, comparisons.

FLOW / STEPS / CYCLE format:
```visual-json
{"type":"flow","title":"Short descriptive title","items":["First step or event","Second step or event","Third step or event"],"note":"Optional one-line explanation"}
```

COMPARE format:
```visual-json
{"type":"compare","title":"Short title","columns":["Option A","Option B"],"rows":[["Point about A","Point about B"],["Another point","Another point"]]}
```

Rules:
- type: must be flow, steps, cycle, or compare
- title: under 80 characters
- each item/label: under 70 characters, must be complete (never cut off)
- flow/steps/cycle: 2 to 6 items
- compare: 2 columns, 2 to 5 rows
- Include only ONE visual-json block per lesson
- Do NOT use if unsure — skip the visual entirely

GRADE-LEVEL DEPTH GUIDE
====================================================

Classes 1-5:
- Very simple vocabulary
- Concrete, everyday examples (home, school, playground)
- Short sentences
- Fun tone with clear progression

Classes 6-8:
- Introduce proper terminology
- Real-world examples
- Some conceptual explanation
- Moderate length

Classes 9-10:
- Full NCERT terminology
- Conceptual depth + exam framing
- Include scientific/logical reasoning
- Exam-oriented language for CBSE board prep

Classes 11-12:
- University-preparation depth
- Full derivations for Science/Maths where relevant
- Connect to board exam and entrance exam patterns (JEE/NEET for Science)
